## Nassau and Suffolk Counties Have Their Own Employment Protections and Complaint Resources

Working in Nassau County isn’t always the same as working in Suffolk County when you’re trying to figure out which employment protections apply. Each county has its own Human Rights Commission and local rules.

### Nassau County: Local Employment-Discrimination Complaint Process

The [Nassau County Commission on Human Rights](https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/3311/Human-Rights-Commission) handles complaints involving employment discrimination. The Commission’s geographic jurisdiction is limited to respondents located within Nassau County. Before we consider whether a complaint can go through the Commission, we need to know where the employer accused of discrimination is located.

### Suffolk County: Local Employment Protections

Under [Suffolk County Code § 528-7](https://ecode360.com/14946832), refusing a reasonable accommodation for an employee’s known pregnancy-related condition can be an unlawful discriminatory practice. If you needed a change at work because of your pregnancy and your employer refused, we can look at what you asked for and why the employer said no. The [Suffolk County Human Rights Commission](https://hrc.suffolkcountyny.gov/Discrimination) also handles employment discrimination and harassment covered by county law.

## Long Island Employment Law FAQs

### Does It Matter Whether I Work in Nassau or Suffolk County?

Yes. New York and federal employment laws can apply throughout Long Island, but Nassau and Suffolk Counties also have separate local employment protections. Where you work can determine whether one of those county laws applies to your employer.

### Where Can a Long Island Employee Report Workplace Discrimination?

The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission and New York State Division of Human Rights accept qualifying employment-discrimination complaints. Nassau and Suffolk Counties also have Human Rights Commissions that handle certain local complaints within their jurisdictions.

### What Records Should I Keep if I Think My Employer Is Preparing to Fire Me?

Keep records you’re entitled to have that document how your employer treated your work before firing became a concern. Earlier performance reviews can show your work history, while disciplinary notices can show when your employer first documented a problem.

### What Can I Do if My Employer Retaliates After I Complain to HR?

We first need to know what you reported to HR because not every workplace complaint receives the same legal protection. We can then review what your employer changed after the complaint and why it says that change was made.

### Can You Represent Me if I Work on Long Island but My Company’s Headquarters Are Somewhere Else?

Potentially. Having headquarters outside Long Island doesn’t prevent us from reviewing your case. Where you performed your job and where your employer made the decision affecting your employment can help determine which laws apply and whether we can represent you.
