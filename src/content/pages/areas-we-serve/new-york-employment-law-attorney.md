## Why Your Employer Fired You Can Determine Which Law Applies

If you were fired, we need to know what your employer says led to the firing. A termination based on unlawful discrimination can fall under [New York Executive Law § 296](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296). If you lost your job after reporting conduct you reasonably believed was illegal, [New York Labor Law § 740](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/740) may apply instead.

If the problem is that your employer didn’t pay you correctly, [Article 6 of the New York Labor Law](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/A6) contains provisions governing the payment of wages. The law we need to examine won’t necessarily be the same one we’d use to determine whether your firing was unlawful.

## You Can Face Discrimination While You Are Still Employed

You can be discriminated against at work even when you are still employed. [New York Executive Law § 296](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/296) applies to more than hiring and firing. It also prohibits specified discrimination in the terms and conditions of your employment, which means losing your job isn’t the only reason discriminatory conduct can violate New York law.

You might still have the same position while a supervisor repeatedly makes insulting comments about your religion. If those comments are tied to a characteristic protected by § 296, we can examine whether the conduct created an unlawful hostile work environment. We don’t need to wait until you’re fired to look at discrimination you’re already dealing with at work.

If your employer owes you money, we need to figure out what you earned and what was actually included in your pay. If you spoke up about something you believed was illegal, we need to know exactly what you reported.

### Your Employer May Still Owe You Wages

Maybe you earned a commission for a completed sale, but it never appeared in your pay. [Article 6 of the New York Labor Law](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/A6) contains requirements governing the payment of wages. We can look at what you earned and what your employer paid to determine whether money is still owed. Your New York employment lawyer can also determine whether you should have received overtime pay.

### What You Reported Can Determine Whether Whistleblower Law Protects You

You may believe your employer is doing something illegal and decide to report it. [New York Labor Law § 740](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/740) can protect you from retaliation when you report conduct you reasonably believe violates a law, rule, or regulation. The statute also covers qualifying reports involving a substantial and specific danger to public health or safety. Calling something unfair isn’t enough by itself; what you reported has to meet the requirements of § 740.

## New York Employment Law FAQs

### Which Employment Laws Protect Workers in New York?

New York employees don’t rely on one employment law for every workplace problem. The New York State Human Rights Law prohibits covered discrimination and harassment, and federal law may separately protect an employee from the same conduct.

### Can I Sue My Employer for Discrimination in New York?

A lawsuit may be available for unlawful discrimination, but not every claim follows the same path to court. The law involved determines whether an agency filing or another procedural requirement needs to be dealt with first.

### Can Salaried Employees Receive Overtime Pay in New York?

Yes. Receiving a salary doesn’t automatically eliminate an employee’s right to overtime pay. An employer relying on an overtime exemption must be able to satisfy the applicable requirements concerning the employee’s actual duties and compensation.

### What Protects Whistleblowers From Retaliation in New York?

New York Labor Law § 740 can protect employees who report or object to employer activity they reasonably believe violates a law, rule, or regulation. What the employee reported or opposed is part of deciding whether § 740 covers the conduct.

### How Long Do I Have to Bring an Employment Claim in New York?

There isn’t one filing deadline for all New York employment claims. A discrimination claim pursued through an agency, for example, may operate under a different deadline than another employment claim filed in court. The specific claim determines which deadline controls.

### Do New York Employment Laws Protect Employees of Small Businesses?

Some do. New York employment laws don’t all define a covered employer the same way. Before assuming a business is too small for a particular law to apply, its size has to be checked against that law’s coverage requirements.
